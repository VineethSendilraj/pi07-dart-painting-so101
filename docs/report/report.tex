\textbf{Summary.} We fine-tune $\pi_{0.7}$~\cite{pi07} to aim a motorized dart blaster held by an SO-101 arm at any cell of a 5$\times$5 grid, given the instruction \texttt{shoot $\langle$cell$\rangle$}. A language model (Claude) sits above the policy, as in hierarchical VLA systems~\cite{hirobot}: it chooses cells to draw a picture, checks the board after each shot and re-issues misses. The policy is trained on 116 teleoperated demonstrations (16 minutes) collected in one session. We release the data in LeRobot format~\cite{lerobot}, with the code, wiring and all fine-tuning logs.

\section{Setup}
The gripper is replaced by a wire loop around the blaster handle, and an ESP32-driven relay replaces the trigger switch, so firing is a binary action. Observations are two 640$\times$480 images and five joint angles at 30\,Hz; actions are five leader-arm joint targets and the trigger. At inference we execute 25 steps of each 50-step chunk, cap joint speed at $3^\circ$ per step, and fire once the arm is still and the predicted trigger exceeds 0.5 for three steps.

\section{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Schedule matters most.} Error is lowest at about 33 passes on the standard base and 18 on the alternative base; longer training overfits.
\item \textbf{More data beat cleaner data.} Dropping the 15 takes recorded before the final crosshair did not help.
\item \textbf{Batch size and oversampling did not help} on the standard base.
\item \textbf{The policy follows the instruction.} With images and joint state held fixed and only the prompt's cell changed, all 24 probed checkpoints aimed in column and row order.
\item \textbf{The trigger is the weak point.} Each take continues 2\,s after the shot with the trigger at 0, so the policy rarely emitted a clean pulse offline; the stillness rule above compensates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
We did not measure a real-world hit rate; evidence is validation error, the prompt probe and a successful live demonstration. Camera and board positions were fixed, and coverage is uneven (two takes each for row 1, A3 and E3).
